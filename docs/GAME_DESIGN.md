# Unidentified Astral Program: game design

A first-person 3D browser game, about 15 minutes (in current iteration), that plays through one night of contact as experiencers describe it. Every scene, line and mechanic is traced to a reported account in [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md).

## Pillars

1. **Faithful.** Nothing is invented where a report exists. Where the game does invent a connection, the field notes say so.
2. **Undecided.** The game never says whether the visitors are teachers, wardens, a breeding programme or a dream. The player's own stance decides the ending, as it does in the real community.
3. **The body is the controller.** Paralysis, breath, gaze and attention are the mechanics. There is no combat and no inventory.
4. **Memory is the reward.** Fighting costs you the memory of the night. Staying aware lets you keep it. This matches the reports, where resisters are switched off and wake with missing time.

## Structure

The game follows Bullard's eight-episode order.

### Act 1: 3:00 AM (capture)

You wake in bed and can only turn your head.

- Turn your head to the clock: 2:59. It ticks to 3:00. For a moment the digits are unreadable shapes.
- A dog barks outside, then stops. Every sound drains away. A ringing tone rises.
- A headlight-like glow sweeps the window and settles into a blue-white glare. Blue mist at the glass.
- A doorway opens in the far wall, outlined in blue. Two small greys walk through in step. Behind them a tall mantis in a purple high-collared robe ducks under the ceiling and stands back.
- The greys raise their hands. The bed vibrates.

**Prompt: BREAK FREE?** Three ways through:

| What you do | What happens | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mash the key | The camera shakes; your right hand twitches with each press and the arm comes up off the covers as the struggle builds; a fist lifts, your voice comes back as a croak, you sit up. The greys look shocked. One comes round to the bedside with a thin silver wand, climbs onto the bed beside you on their knees, eyes on you, and brings the red light at its tip to a hand's width from your eyes. Electricity, then black. On a repeat night a voice asks, wearily, "Why do we have to go through this every time?" | Fist and voice, shocked greys, the wand, the electricity and the line are each from separate Reddit accounts; the field notes say they are combined |
| Do nothing | Fear rises on its own. You are taken, frightened, and the next act opens with you on the table. | The standard report |
| Breathe slowly (hold and release in rhythm with the hum of their raised hands, which swells and fades; your chest and view rise with each breath, and a grey talks the first breaths through) | Fear falls. A voice says "It's fine, you are safe." You are taken calm, and you keep more of the night. | Jalal's relaxation method; Phantoms & Monsters line |

Breaking free leads straight to the **Missing Time** ending: you wake at 4:14 facing the wrong way in bed, with three marks by your thumb and no memory. The game tells you nothing more and offers the night again. Players who want the ship must stop fighting.

### Act 2: through the wall (transit)

You rise until your nose is inches from the ceiling texture. The wall turns translucent, like a hologram, and you pass through it into a tunnel that pulls "like an ocean current". Looking back, you see your own body in the bed. The game never resolves whether you left it.

### Act 3: the white room (examination)

A round, seamless, glowing room with no lamp and no door. You lie on a pedestal table.

- Small greys work around you. A mechanical eye slides from the wall and scans you.
- Another human lies on the next table, eyes open, staring upward.
- A tall grey leans in until their eyes fill the view.

**Mechanic: the stare.** Hold their gaze while fear pushes your view away. Hold it and a night sky opens out of each of their eyes; look away too often and you are sedated, losing part of the next act's memory. This is Jacobs's mindscan with Barney Hill's line.

Afterwards a "static fuzz" sweeps up the walls and the room looks slightly different. The game does not say which version was real.

### Act 4: the crossroads (tour and conference)

You can walk. A round metal room with four doorways, and the mantis on a dais in a column of light. The doors stay shut until the mantis asks "Do you trust us?" and you answer. Visit the rooms in any order; each one seals once you have been inside.

**The classroom.** A grey you somehow recognise hands you a star toy.
- *Lesson 1, the sky from home:* rotate a field of stars until Orion lines up as seen from Earth.
- *Lesson 2, the orb:* a glowing orb hovers over a raised dais. By steady attention you turn it into a blue cube, then a purple pyramid, then move it. It responds while your aim is slow and steady, and pushes back when you grab at it. A ring round it fills while your eyes rest on it, a faint line points back to it when you lose it, and each change of shape is seen happening. The teacher says "Don't be afraid that it won't work. You're holding yourself back." A second grey says "just have them take it again."

**The window.** A wall-sized screen shows the living world, then what was done to it: a silent flash, mushroom clouds, a firestorm, a dead plain. The film fades and the Earth is below you: it was never a screen. You walk to the glass and lay your hand flat on it. The mantis stands beside you and says "I know this is hard. I'm sorry." You choose how to answer: grief, anger at being shown it, or "why show me?" Mack's cases, with Turner's suspicion available as a reply.

**The hall of containers.** Rows of softly lit vessels. A listless child holds out a toy. You may take it. It will not come home with you. Kept brief and quiet. Hopkins and Jacobs, with Lazar's contested "containers" line on a wall panel.

**The still room.** The Gateway exercise. You lie on a low table: "Mind awake. Body asleep."
1. **Tuning:** a different tone plays in each ear. Steer the difference between them to 4 Hz and hold it while it drifts.
2. **Rolling out:** roll and lift out of your body.
3. **Out:** you are under your own bedroom ceiling, looking down at the sleeper in your bed. The small grey by the bed looks up. "Enough."

Robert Monroe's Focus 10 and rolling out, and the 1983 US Army Gateway report. Whose body is in the bed stays open, as in Act 2.

### Act 4b: the control room (behind the scenes)

After all four rooms the mantis asks, "Would you like to see more?" (if you have shown no trust at all, trust −2 or less, they say "You are not ready." instead and you wake to the Scepticism ending). Say no and you wake to the Scepticism ending. Say yes and they unfold an arm and lay one long finger in your open hand. A hum rises, the floor trembles, and the round room comes apart around you into a far larger one.

One operation, seen three ways. Tonight a group in a field in the countryside is doing CE5, meditating together and signalling with a green laser to invite contact.
- **The loom.** A domed hall with a basin of lives below a gallery. A mantis in a robe of shifting silver weaves threads of light with six arms. Four beads each hold one way the group's night can go: they go home, nothing comes, rain, fog. Your view leaves your body and circles them slowly. You can hold one; their head turns all the way round to you, like an owl's. "The living do not touch the weave." With enough trust your touch pulls the chosen thread tight.
- **The weather.** A tall grey in a high-collared cloak works a relief table of the countryside: a cloud forms in clear air, a lever pushes out a pointed bank of mist (Travis Walton's lever), rain falls on one farm. Their fingers rest on your hand as you press the last pad.
- **The field.** You lie on a couch under a ring of light and wake inside a maroon-robed mantis at the edge of the field, beside two greys: all three drawn in lines of light. You saw the group arrive and set up. The mantis sees in four dimensions: each person trails where they have been and where they are going, and for a moment the whole scene folds. The laser draws a triangle and three lights answer. The friends freeze. You switch into one of them, paralysed, eyes only: the lights split and dart, fog comes over the hedge in a straight, steered line, and in it stand a mantis and two greys like half-present silver. "It is you." You go back into the mantis once, hold out the gold medallion from your robe, blazing, and through his eyes again he is lifted rigid off the blanket into the sky.
- **Afterwards.** In the basin he lies on a white table: the man from the next table in the exam room. "Hours ago. Or not yet."

Sources: Steven Greer's CE-5 practice; three lights answering a traced triangle (his group at Gulf Breeze, 1992); rain falling on one small field (his group in Wiltshire, July 1992); Travis Walton's lever; being shown things by a tall mantis (Linda Porter, one of John Mack's cases) and being aware from two points at once (an experiencer's post). The field notes give each.

**The child.** Only if you took the toy: a warm nursery, and the copper-haired child from the classroom and the hall of containers, who does a small, happy dance from side to side, clapping, (a hypnosis account of meeting a hybrid daughter). When the toy touches their hand, three memories come back, tied together by one hummed tune: the hall of containers, where the grey was watching you; the classroom, where the child held your stare, humming under their breath; and a night years ago, with a grey in a black wig humming that tune, "Don't be afraid. I'm like you." (the wig is from an account where a grey in a black bob looked ridiculous and the experiencer was less afraid; the link to children is from David Huggins's accounts). Then: "This child is yours." Reach for them, step back, or say nothing. They keep the toy "till you come back."

Either way, the beings now want you seen higher up: "Tonight you were inside one of us. No one does that and stays only what they were." or "You have a child among us now. Those above us have asked to see the child's parent."

### Act 5: the mandala (otherworldly journey and theophany)

The mantis lays one long finger in your open hand.

- The ringing returns and climbs. A crinkling sound.
- **The waiting room.** A black-and-white checkered palace. Jester faces press out of the walls, sticking out their tongues, turning slowly from frown to grin.
- **Mechanic: look past them.** Keep your eyes on the far end of the hall. Jester faces swell out of the walls to catch your eye; look straight at one and it shouts an insult ("I've got you now!"), your focus drains and fear rises. Every so often one tears itself out of the wall and flies at you, stopping a few centimetres from your eyes to taunt you, then backs away into the dark. Keep looking ahead and focus builds until the palace chars and burns away. One jester only points down the hall, as if it wants you to go on; reports disagree on whether jesters block or guide, and the game keeps both.
- **The chrysanthemum.** A pink, purple and green mandala grows until it fills the view. An arched door forms in its centre, with more doors behind it. They open.
- The mantis walks out through the doors towards you, their skin glowing green and their robe shifting colour, and takes hold of you as the last words arrive.
- **The council.** Darkness and nine orbs of light. A rush of word fragments you cannot hold, then four lines from the orbs.

The message arrives in pieces you choose between, and the last choice sets the ending:

- **"Is that all this is?"** They answer: "Well, what else were you expecting?"
- **"Thank you."**
- **"I don't believe this. Let me past."** You try to go past them, through the mandala. A calm voice: "You shouldn't be here. Come back later."
- **Say nothing, and remember.**

### Act 6: return and aftermath

You wake. What you find depends on the night.

## Endings

| Ending | How | What you wake to | Reading it echoes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Missing Time** | Broke free in Act 1 | 4:14, wrong way round in bed, three marks by the thumb, no memory | Hopkins, Jacobs |
| **Communion** | Lucidity 0.55 or more and trust 2 or more, then said thank you or nothing | Morning (8:08 on the clock), full memory, a small praying mantis climbs onto the sill outside the window and turns to look in | Mack, Strieber |
| **The Wardens** | Challenged them or tried to pass the mandala | Thrown back. 3:00 again. Was the answer real, or what you already believed? | Turner, the prison-planet community, the 2024 DMT trip report |
| **Hang Up the Phone** | "Is that all this is?", or thank you / nothing with lucidity kept but trust under 2 | You wake laughing and angry. "You can be whoever and do whatever you want." The clock ticks to 3:01. "I'm coming back later, to check again." | DMT trip report, r/Experiencers and r/DMT, 2024 |
| **Scepticism** | Lucidity under 0.55 at the council, "No" when the mantis offers more, or no trust shown at all (not offered) | Morning. A calm voice explains sleep paralysis. There is a scoop mark on your shin | Clancy and French, against Hopkins |

## Systems

- **Fear** (hidden, shown only through camera shake when it is high, heartbeat and vignette). Rises with struggle and with looking at what frightens you; falls with slow breathing, holding the stare, and keeping your eyes on the way ahead in the jester palace.
- **Lucidity** (hidden). How much you will remember. Gained by staying calm, holding the stare, the orb, "It's alive" at the window, the still room and saying nothing at the council. Lost by freezing, looking away from the stare and doubting at the window. Below 0.55, on the ship and in the vision, details will not hold: while you look away, door marks, writing, the children, the Moon, the forms in the vessels and the council orbs change (the lucid-dream check); a detail you hold in view may warp, stretching and wavering, and change in front of you; and on the ship, now and then, the whole view blurs while you struggle to remain lucid. At the council it decides between Communion and Scepticism.
- **Trust.** Earned by breathing, holding the stare, "Yes", "It's alive", taking the toy and "Thank you"; lost by "No", the rude thought, doubting at the window and pushing past the council. It changes the mantis's lines, whether you are offered the vision, and Communion vs Hang Up the Phone. Exact numbers: `DECISIONS.md`.
- **Field notes.** Each motif you witness unlocks a short entry naming who reported it and how well sourced it is. Open with Tab. This is where the faithfulness is visible, and it gives a reason to replay.

## Controls

Mouse to look (arrow keys also turn the head); W A S D to walk when you can (Shift to run); Space to struggle; hold Shift as the hum swells and let go as it fades to breathe; E to interact; number keys for dialogue; Tab for field notes; P or Esc to pause; F3 for a frame-rate readout; F2 for developer mode (speed and scene jumps). Touch controls appear on touch screens.

## Look and sound

- **Bedroom:** dark, warm lamp tones overtaken by blue-white. A red LED clock.
- **Ship:** white seamless curves, sourceless glow, faint water-like shimmer on the walls.
- **Greys:** small, large-headed, black almond eyes, moving in exact step.
- **Mantis:** about 8 ft, triangular green head, slanted black eyes with star points, purple robe with a collar above their head, gold script on the seams, a gold sun-like medallion, three-fingered hands, long stillness broken by sharp head tilts, clicking when they "speak".
- **Mandala space:** checkerboard black and white, then saturated pink, purple and green.
- **Sound:** all synthesised. The rising carrier tone, the hum that drains other sound away, clicking between the beings, a heartbeat tied to fear (it races as you look at more of the jester faces), long open-mouthed tones in the mandala.
- **Text:** telepathy appears as whole phrases at once, never typed out letter by letter.

## Technology

- Three.js in the browser, plain JavaScript modules, no build step.
- All models are built in code and all audio is synthesised at run time. The only image files are three public-domain NASA maps of the Earth and Moon.
- Runs from any static web host (itch.io as an HTML game, GitHub Pages and so on) or locally with the included zero-dependency server and start script.
- Target: any recent browser with WebGL 2 and import maps (iOS 16.4 or newer on iPhone and iPad), a mouse and a keyboard. Phones and tablets work too, with on-screen touch controls shown from the start.

## Scope

In: the six acts and the control-room act (loom, weather, the field, the child), the endings, four ship rooms, six mechanics (break free, breathe, the stare, the two lessons, the Gateway tuning, look past the jesters), field notes.

Out for the first version: saved games beyond unlocked field notes, voice acting, a second night.

## Content notes

The title screen warns of flashing lights, loud tones, strong language, paralysis and medical-examination themes, and says that every scene is built from what people report. Examination scenes show no injury and nothing sexual. The hall of containers is suggestive, not graphic. No drug use is shown; the mandala sequence happens on the ship.

## Design decisions

1. **Breaking free ends the run early.** Struggling earns the wand and the blackout, and costs the memory of the night. The alternative considered was to zap the player and carry them aboard anyway.
2. **The jester and mandala sequence happens on the ship**, led by the mantis, with no drug in the story. The alternative considered was a separate chapter.
3. **Tone of the mantis.** The mantis is written as ambiguous: loving if you trust them, evasive if you press them.
